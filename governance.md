## Session 1 — the six questions

| # | Question | Our answer (provisional) | To check with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **EU AI Act:** which risk tier is our agent in, and why? (minimal · limited — transparency: tell the user it is an AI · high-risk · prohibited) | **Likely minimal risk**, because the ML agent predicts occupancy and recommends last-minute offers for sports studios, fitness centres and courts. It does not make decisions about employment, credit or access to essential services. This is a preliminary assessment based on the planned use case, not a formal legal classification. If the product includes an AI chatbot or other AI-generated interactions, separate transparency obligations may apply. | Company legal/compliance team; confirm the final features and intended use against the EU AI Act. [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) |
| 2 | **Data:** whose data does it use, and on what legal basis? (GDPR art. 6; what the B2B contract allows) | The agent may use partner data such as schedules, capacity, booking history, cancellations, prices, location, activity type, instructor and seasonality. Personal data may be involved if records identify customers or instructors. Prefer aggregated or pseudonymised data where possible. For personal data, the company and partners must identify the appropriate GDPR Article 6 legal basis and define their roles, permitted uses, retention and data-sharing rules in their contracts. A B2B contract alone does not automatically establish a GDPR legal basis. | Company data protection officer or legal team; partner studios and facilities; review data-processing and data-sharing agreements. [EDPB: Legal basis](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/topics/key-gdpr-concepts/legal-basis_en) |
| 3 | **Accountability:** who is accountable when it is wrong, and where is the human? | The agent provides predictions and recommendations, but a designated company or partner manager remains responsible for approving offers in the initial version. The company operating the platform is responsible for its system and processes, while each partner controls its own commercial constraints and approved discounts, according to the agreed responsibilities. Record predictions, recommendations, approvals and published offers so errors can be investigated. | Company product owner; partner managers; legal/compliance team. Define responsibility in the B2B agreement and operational workflow. |
| 4 | **Never:** what must it never do? (our first guardrail) | Never publish an offer or change a price without the required authorisation. Never exceed a partner's minimum price or maximum discount, invent available capacity, or recommend a discount without checking the latest booking information. Never use customer or instructor personal data for unrelated purposes, expose confidential partner data, or discriminate against people based on sensitive personal characteristics. | Company product owner; partner managers; data protection officer. Agree on hard pricing limits, data-access controls and approval requirements. |
| 5 | **Explainability:** explainable to whom: the customer, the support agent, the auditor? | **Partner studios and company operators:** show why a slot was flagged, the predicted occupancy or empty-slot probability, relevant factors such as day, time, seasonality and booking pace, and the proposed discount. **Customers:** clearly display the offer price, conditions, availability and cancellation policy. **Support and auditors:** keep logs of data inputs, recommendations, rule checks, approvals and final actions. Explain predictions as estimates, not guarantees. | Product/design team; partner managers; customer support; compliance team. Test whether users understand the recommendations and offer conditions. |
| 6 | **The people it cannot serve:** what happens to them? | New studios or facilities with little booking history may not have enough data for reliable ML predictions. They should still be able to use the platform through simple rules, manual recommendations or a gradual onboarding period while data accumulates. Facilities with incomplete data should receive a clear warning rather than a misleading prediction. Customers who do not use the app or cannot access last-minute offers should retain access to partners' normal booking channels. | Partner managers; customer support; accessibility and product teams. Test the fallback process with new partners, low-data facilities and customers who do not use the app. |
